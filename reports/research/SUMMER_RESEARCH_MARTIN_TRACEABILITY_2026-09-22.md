## What the project is actually trying to build

Martin's words describe a **nationwide US candidate-screening system**: Sentinel-2 is the inference input, valid time-aligned EMIT methane observations provide supervision, and the output is a spatial plume/candidate map that can be reviewed against known CAFOs, oil sites, and other possible sources. The CAFO inventory is an acquisition and corroboration frame. It is not a date-specific methane label. This wording is an interpretation of the meeting notes, because the notes do not define a complete label schema or a final operating threshold.

The work therefore has three separate tracks:

1. **Plume mapping:** learn a Sentinel-2 target/reference model against quantitative or physically supported EMIT plume targets, with unknown support excluded from loss and metrics.
2. **Facility recovery:** measure whether observable known facilities are covered by ranked candidates using geographic rotations and a reviewed control set. Facility recovery is not methane precision/recall.
3. **Nationwide review:** run the frozen Sentinel-only model over the US, retain candidate geometry and provenance, then review listed and unlisted sources. An unlisted detection is a review candidate, not an automatic false positive or true emission.

TESSERA belongs after the Sentinel baseline is frozen. Its annual representation can be useful for retrospective facility/land-use recognition, but it is not methane-label data and can leak future information into a date-specific plume task.

## What was promising, and what was not

The earlier MARS systems contain useful benchmark and scene-ranking evidence, but they do not establish a Sentinel-to-EMIT upscaler. The released MARS checkpoint and its recovered dense examples remain the correct historical comparison. The Gaussian/DOFA family produced small development ranking gains and then failed the strict superiority gate. The temporal-attention implementation passes a four-example synthetic fitting diagnostic, but no real imagery result exists. The Model6 multiscale pilots are real training/inference runs, yet their pooled reviewed-MARS IoU is only 1.33% and 2.67%, with 8.90% and 5.06% mean negative-pixel activation; the constrained follow-up covers only 7.28% of positive EMIT support. These numbers are in the [pilot design status](../../docs/GTM_Model6_multiscale_reconstruction_design.md) and the [Model6 journal](../../research/GTM_Model6/GTM_Model6_journal.md). Neither pilot is promoted.

The exact previous-team checkpoint is now hash-pinned and the comparison harness passes its focused tests. Runtime verification still cannot load that Keras model here: only about 4.4 GiB was available during the check, TensorFlow is absent, and the baseline contract requires at least 6 GiB plus TensorFlow. Hash verification is therefore evidence of artifact identity, not a new legacy prediction.

The proposed dual-head implementation now exists in [`GTM_Model6_mapper.py`](../../research/GTM_Model6/GTM_Model6_mapper.py): four independently parameterized branches consume exact native S16/S32/S64/S128 contexts, emit a plume logit and a signed-log enhancement field, and fuse branch logits by an explicit arithmetic mean. Its two-example CPU smoke passed the repaired contract (24 steps, seed 20260922, 1,313,720 parameters, training-only IoU 1.0, enhancement log1p MAE 0.0595). This is a wiring diagnostic only; it opened no imagery or holdout. The bounded real-data follow-up is separate: the one-fold MARS diagnostic completed with fixed-threshold held IoU 0.0 and an all-negative output, so it is retained as engineering failure evidence rather than a result.

## Best-supported next architecture

The next scientific model should be a **multiscale Sentinel-to-EMIT plume mapper**, not another unconstrained crop classifier:

- five Sentinel bands at target/reference dates, plus temporal log-ratios, validity and physics channels;
- independent S16/S32/S64/S128 branches with shared preprocessing and explicit native georeferencing;
- a dense plume-probability head and a masked quantitative EMIT enhancement head; unknown EMIT exterior is ignored;
- BCE plus Dice or focal-Tversky for reviewed masks, log-Huber (or an uncertainty-weighted regression loss) for valid quantitative enhancement, and no scene loss that can replace dense quality as the checkpoint criterion;
- uniform branch mean first; a small learned merger only from train-partition out-of-fold predictions;
- synthetic Gaussian/Beer-Lambert or WRF-style plumes for plumbing and pretraining only, followed by real EMIT fine-tuning and index-disjoint transfer checks;
- a Gaussian source head only after source coordinates and wind are verified, evaluated by map-distance error and uncertainty;
- a separate CAFO facility-retrieval classifier/score, with controls and the Martin 20/80 rotations, never conflated with plume segmentation.
